Today's Thesis

The 10-Year Just Cleared 5.10% — Big Stocks Shrugged, Small Companies Didn't

The 10-year Treasury yield, the price of borrowing that sets mortgage rates, jumped 0.11 points to 5.10%, decisively through the 5% line the market had been watching. Two things pushed it. Traders are betting harder that the Fed's next move is a hike, which has the dollar index near a 19-month high at 103.17. Oil also jumped after Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they targeted a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, and Brent rose 3.3% to $95.82. Big stocks slipped only about 0.35%, but the Russell 2000 (small companies) fell 1.66%, and gold dropped 2.88%. The market is treating this as a rates problem, not a fear problem. October home sales at a 16-year low show the cost of money is already biting the real economy.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: yields punch through 5% on Fed hike bets. Secondary: oil bounces back above $95 on Iran.

YIELDS THROUGH 5%
Hike bets and pricier oil are pushing the cost of borrowing higher at the same time.
What changed today is that the 10-year yield closed at 5.10% instead of stalling just under 5%, while the dollar rose to near a 19-month high. Higher oil feeds inflation fears, inflation fears feed Fed hike bets, and hike bets lift yields. That chain is why gold fell 2.88%: gold pays no interest, so when safe Treasuries pay over 5% and the dollar strengthens, holding it costs more. Housing shows where the bill lands. Existing home sales hit a 16-year low with mortgage rates near a three-year high.
This lasts until the 10-year closes back under 5.00%, and until then a Fed rate cut is the thing to bet against, not on. The damage so far is concentrated in small companies and housing, not the big indexes.
OIL BOUNCES BACK
A reported Iranian strike on a US radar in Bahrain sent Brent back above $95.
Brent rose 3.3% to $95.82 and WTI rose 2.55% to $88.74, reversing a slide toward the lowest prices since August. Oil is still on track for a second weekly decline, because the blockade on Iran is holding, so this reads as a bounce inside a downtrend rather than a new trend. It matters mostly because it came at the worst moment for inflation expectations, adding fuel to the bond selloff.
Real if Brent closes above $95 again and more attacks follow; temporary if it fades back toward $90 without a new incident.
The Core Dynamic

Higher Rates Are Moving From Markets to Main Street

The 10-year yield is the wholesale price of money, and mortgages, car loans and business loans are all priced off it with a delay. October's home-sales collapse is the bill arriving. Stocks haven't fully paid it yet, which is why small companies, which lean harder on borrowed money, are flinching first.

Directional Read

The variable that decides this is the 10-year yield. If it holds above 5.10% and pushes toward 5.25%, the big indexes' calm becomes hard to defend and the 25,000 Nasdaq line comes under real pressure. If it slips back under 5.00%, today reads as an oil-driven overshoot. Until oil and the dollar both calm down, assume borrowing costs stay high and the pain keeps showing up in small caps and housing first.

Scenario A — Overshoot fades: Brent slides back toward $90 as the blockade keeps supply tight and no new attacks come, hike bets cool, and the 10-year closes back under 5.00%.
Scenario B — Rates break stocks: Another oil spike and a 10-year close above 5.25% would push the Nasdaq below 25,000 and the VIX toward 25 as the big indexes finally catch up to small caps.
