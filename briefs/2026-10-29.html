Today's Thesis

Big Tech Took the Hit While Falling Yields Lifted Everything Smaller

The Nasdaq fell 1.38% to 26,185 while the Russell 2000 (small companies) rose 1.01%, so this was a rotation out of the biggest tech names, not a market-wide retreat. Investors punished Alphabet after earnings and let Microsoft off the hook, which tells you they are now judging AI spenders one by one. At the same time, the 10-year Treasury yield slipped to 4.99% (-0.06 pts), back under 5% despite fast growth (3.4% annualized in the third quarter) and inflation at 3.7%. Cheaper borrowing helped smaller companies and did nothing for the mega-caps, so the S&P 500 lost only 0.3% while its heaviest stocks dragged.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: tech earnings split the AI winners from the losers. Secondary: bond buyers defended 5%.

TECH EARNINGS SPLIT
Investors punished Alphabet and spared Microsoft, so the AI spending story is no longer one trade.
The Nasdaq lost 1.38% while the Russell 2000 gained 1.01%, a gap of roughly 2.4 percentage points, which shows money moving out of mega-cap tech rather than out of stocks altogether. With a 10-year Treasury paying about 5%, buyers want proof that heavy AI spending turns into profit, and the market is now rewarding or punishing each company on that evidence. That is why the Nasdaq sits about 2% from the 26,713 repair level instead of closing in on it.
Expect more sorting among the big AI spenders while yields stay near 5%; the Nasdaq reclaiming 26,713 now needs a few strong earnings reactions in a row, and losing 25,500 (about 2.6% below today's close) would mean the sorting has turned into selling.
BONDS DEFEND 5%
The 10-year yield fell back under 5% even though growth and inflation both came in hot.
Growth of 3.4% and inflation of 3.7% normally push yields up, since they keep the Fed on its December hike path, yet buyers stepped in at 5% and the 10-year closed at 4.99%. Lagarde's statement that the ECB is ready to intervene if markets need it likely eased nerves across global bond markets, though the exact trigger is unclear. Falling yields cut borrowing costs for smaller companies, which fits the Russell's 1% gain.
Treat it as real only if the 10-year closes under 5% again and the 30-year stays below 5.50%; a close back above 5.10% would say today was a pause inside the climb.
The Core Dynamic

At 5% Rates, Investors Want Receipts, Not Promises

When a safe government bond pays about 5%, investors stop paying up for a big future payoff and ask to see profits now. That turns each earnings report into a verdict on whether AI spending is earning its keep, which is why one giant can fall while another holds. What is different this time is that the pressure lands on the most loved companies in the market, not the weakest.

Directional Read

The variable that decides this week is whether the 10-year yield stays under 5% while big-tech earnings reactions stay selective. If it does, the pullback in the Nasdaq stays a reshuffle and the broader market holds up. If yields push back above 5% with the Fed still leaning toward a December hike, the sorting turns into across-the-board selling of expensive stocks. Right now, the price of money matters more to stocks than the headline growth number.

Scenario A — Reshuffle, not retreat: The 10-year closes under 5% again and the Nasdaq reclaims 26,713 on a strong AI-earnings reaction, which would show the sorting was healthy.
Scenario B — Yields break back up: The 10-year climbs back above 5.10% and the Nasdaq slides toward 25,500, showing the market cannot carry expensive AI stocks at these rates.
