Today's Thesis

The Fed Hiked Straight Into an Oil War It Can't Fix

The Fed raised its benchmark rate by a quarter of a percentage point, to a range of 3.75%-4.00% — its first hike in three years, and a unanimous one. The market had already bet this was coming, which is why the reaction was oddly split: the Nasdaq barely moved (-0.01%) while the Dow fell 1.21%, its worst day in this stretch. That split matters more than the hike itself — it suggests the rate-sensitive, industrial side of the market is absorbing the pain while tech is betting the worst uncertainty is now behind it.

What's Actually Driving This

The Fed's hike and the Dow-Nasdaq split that followed it

FED HIKES ANYWAY
The Fed raised rates for the first time in three years, betting it can cool inflation the market says is coming from oil, not demand.
This was fully priced in — the market has been positioned for exactly this move for over a week — which is why the immediate equity reaction was muted rather than violent. But the substance is real: a unanimous vote, a Fed governor calling inflation 'too high and has been for too long,' and a signal that a second hike is likely this year. The tension flagged repeatedly in this theme — a demand-side tool aimed at a supply-side (oil-driven) inflation problem — just became actual policy instead of a forecast.
This is not a one-day story. If oil stays elevated and the Fed still delivers a second hike later this year, it will be tightening into a supply shock it cannot solve with rates — watch whether Brent falls or keeps climbing, because that decides whether today's hike looks smart or reckless in hindsight.
DOW-NASDAQ REVERSAL
Tech shrugged off the hike while the Dow took the hit.
For more than four sessions, the Nasdaq had been underperforming the Dow — tech leading market weakness instead of being a safe harbor. Today flipped: Nasdaq essentially flat, Dow down over a full point, likely because industrials and rate-sensitive names (autos, financials) feel a hike's borrowing-cost sting more directly and immediately than software and AI names do. One session doesn't confirm a trend reversal.
If the Nasdaq keeps outperforming the Dow over the next couple of sessions, this is a real regime shift — tech finding footing now that rate uncertainty is resolved. If the old pattern reasserts itself tomorrow, today was just decision-day noise.
The Core Dynamic

The independence fight stopped being hypothetical today.

Trump publicly demanded the Fed cut rates to '1% or less' on the same day it hiked — turning a slow-building tension into an open, live conflict between the White House and the central bank. That fight doesn't resolve with today's vote; it escalates every time the Fed's next move contradicts what the President wants.

Directional Read

The variable that decides everything from here is whether oil-driven inflation eases on its own or forces the Fed to hike again despite a slowing economy. If it eases, today's hike looks like a one-and-done insurance move. If it doesn't, the Fed is committed to a second hike into what's starting to look like a genuine stagflation setup — rising prices, slowing growth, together. Watch oil before you watch the Fed; oil is what's actually driving the Fed's hand right now.

Scenario A — Oil Breaks, Fed Pauses: Brent crude rolls over below $100 in the coming sessions, taking pressure off headline inflation and giving the Fed room to skip the signaled second hike.
Scenario B — Oil Holds, Fed Hikes Again: Brent grinds higher toward $110 while the Fed still delivers a second hike later this year, confirming the market is now pricing tightening into a supply shock it can't control.
