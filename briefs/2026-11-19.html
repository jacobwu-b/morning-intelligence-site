Today's Thesis

The AI Boom Just Got Blamed for Inflation, and Stocks Took the Hit

The S&P 500 fell 2.02%, the Nasdaq 2.25% and the Dow 2.19%, and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) jumped 15.8% to 28.44. The selling had nothing to do with oil or bond yields, which barely moved. It came from AI itself. Two senior officials, Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee and IMF head Kristalina Georgieva, argued in public that the AI build-out is pushing up prices and interest rates. On the same day, Anthropic, the maker of the Claude AI assistant, finished its first trading session at $97 after a record-breaking IPO. That put a huge new AI stock on the market just as the case for owning AI got harder. Stocks priced for cheap money and AI payoffs are the ones that suffer if AI itself keeps money expensive.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: AI is now an inflation story. Secondary: a record AI listing lands into a falling market.

AI INFLATION FEAR
The AI boom is no longer a hope for lower prices; two senior officials are now calling it a source of higher rates.
Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee tied 'disturbingly high' inflation to the data-center building spree, and IMF head Kristalina Georgieva warned that AI demand will lift interest rates around the world. This lands on a Fed already leaning toward another hike, with Governor Christopher Waller floating a half-point move. Bond yields barely moved, with the 10-year at 5.05%, so the damage showed up in share prices rather than in bonds. Stocks valued on AI's future profits are the most exposed when money stays expensive.
This is a re-pricing of how much AI is worth in a 5% interest-rate world, not a one-day scare. It fades only if the 10-year stays near 5.05% and no other Fed official backs Waller's half-point idea; a 10-year push toward 5.25% would mean it is getting worse.
IPO SUPPLY
A record AI listing arrived just as buyers were already losing their nerve.
Anthropic, valued near a trillion dollars, closed its first day at $97 as the market fell sharply. A listing that size forces investors to sell other holdings to make room, which likely amplified the drop. Gold's 1.51% fall on a day of flat dollar and flat yields fits the same picture of investors selling whatever they can to raise cash, though that is an inference, not something the market has confirmed.
If Anthropic holds near $97 while the broader market steadies, the IPO was a side effect; a close below $90 would show that appetite for AI stock itself is cracking.
The Core Dynamic

AI Is Competing for the Same Money It Needs Cheap

The AI build-out is now bidding against everyone else for money, electricity, concrete and workers, so it is lifting the very prices and interest rates that AI stocks need to stay low. What makes this episode different is the direction: AI was supposed to be the force that eased inflation, and it is now being cast as a cause of it.

Directional Read

The deciding variable is the 10-year Treasury yield, which sits at 5.05%. If it holds there while stocks fall, this is a valuation reset that can burn itself out; if it climbs toward 5.25%, the selloff has a bond-market engine behind it and the S&P 500's next stop is lower. Don't buy a bounce until yields confirm the pressure has stopped.

Scenario A — Digestion: The 10-year stays near 5.05%, Anthropic holds near $97, and the VIX slides back toward 25, which would mean today was forced selling rather than a change in outlook.
Scenario B — Re-pricing: Another Fed official backs a half-point hike and the 10-year pushes toward 5.25%, driving the S&P 500 under 7,000 and the VIX through 30.
