Today's Thesis

Wall Street Just Priced in What a Democratic Washington Could Do to AI, and It Did So in One Afternoon

The Nasdaq fell 5.52% to 24,315, its worst day in 19 months, pushing it into correction territory (down 10% or more from its peak). The S&P 500 lost 4.36% and the VIX, the market's fear gauge, jumped almost 70% to 28.18. The trigger was a rethink of AI and chip stocks, whose prices assume data centers keep getting built with little interference. A Democratic sweep, plus voters in several places backing data-center moratoriums, put that assumption in doubt, and roughly $2 trillion of Nasdaq value went with it. The usual cushion is missing: inflation is 3.7%, the Bank of England hiked today and Fed officials are talking about hikes, so nobody is signaling relief for stocks.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: a political shock to the AI trade. Secondary: central banks that are tightening, not rescuing.

AI POLITICAL SHOCK
The AI trade was priced for unlimited building, and the election made limits plausible.
AI and chip stocks have led the market because investors assumed data centers, power and chips would keep expanding with little friction. Voters backing data-center moratoriums and a Democratic Congress likely to take a harder look at the industry put a ceiling on that assumption for the first time. The selling fed on itself: retail investors dumped more stock in one day than at any point since the Covid sell-off, and the VIX jumped almost 70%.
Expect jumpy trading until the VIX is back under 25. A Nasdaq close below 24,000 would signal that investors are repricing AI itself, not just panicking.
NO RATE CUSHION
The institutions that usually soften a sell-off are tightening instead.
The Bank of England raised its rate to 4% from 3.75% and signaled another rise, with inflation heading toward 5% by mid-2027. Hammack and Barkin both favor gradual Fed hikes. Oil also bounced, with Brent up 3.88% to $98.29, so the inflation worry that drives those hikes has not gone away. Bonds offered only a little shelter, with the 10-year yield down just 0.06 points to 4.84%.
Watch Brent against $100 and the 10-year against 5.00%. If both break higher while stocks are weak, the market loses its last cushion.
The Core Dynamic

A stock price is a bet on what you will be allowed to build

Think of a restaurant chain valued on its plan to open 500 more locations. If the city council starts debating a cap on permits, the chain's value drops overnight even though this week's sales haven't changed. AI stocks are priced on permission to keep expanding, so a hint of limits reprices them instantly. Many investors held the same crowded position and tried to leave together. What makes this different from a typical political sell-off is that rate cuts normally come to the rescue, and with inflation at 3.7% and central banks hiking, that rescue isn't coming.

Historical Precedent

Regulatory scares in tech either pass in weeks or expose how expensive the market already was, and the difference is whether central banks help

2000
In April 2000, a federal judge ruled that Microsoft had illegally abused its monopoly, and Microsoft's stock fell about 15% that day. The Nasdaq lost roughly 35% between its March peak and mid-April, bounced, and eventually fell nearly 80% by 2002. The Fed was raising rates through that spring, so there was no relief from policy.
A regulatory scare rarely pops a bubble on its own; it exposes one, especially when the central bank is tightening.
2024
On August 5, 2024, the Nasdaq fell about 3.4% and the VIX spiked as a crowded, borrowed-money trade unwound. Within a few weeks the S&P 500 had recovered most of the drop. The Fed was about to start cutting rates, which gave investors permission to buy the dip.
Panic driven by positioning clears fast, but only when the central bank is on your side.
Directional Read

The variable that decides what comes next is oil. It feeds inflation, which is what keeps the Fed and the Bank of England leaning toward hikes. If Brent stays under $100, this is a stock-valuation problem that can burn out as forced selling ends. If Brent climbs back through $100, it becomes a stocks-and-bonds problem with no safety net. Until oil settles, treat bounces as relief rallies, not as proof that the low is in.

Scenario A — Fear burns out: Brent stays under $100, the 10-year stays below 4.90%, and the Nasdaq closes back above 25,000 within days as the VIX falls back toward 25.
Scenario B — Repricing deepens: Brent closes above $100 and pushes the 10-year toward 5.00% while the Nasdaq breaks below 24,000, which would mean AI valuations are being reset and rate hikes are back on the table.
