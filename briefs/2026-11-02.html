Today's Thesis

Oil Just Fell 11% and Stocks Still Couldn't Rally

Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, dropped 10.9% to $104.77 and US crude fell 7.4% to $96.46 on hopes that Iran war talks lead somewhere. Cheaper oil is exactly the relief this market has wanted, yet the Nasdaq still fell 1.07% and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) rose 8.9% to 21.15. The reason is that the sell-off that began October 30 is now about whether AI spending will ever pay for itself, and cheaper gasoline does nothing for that. The Dow rose 0.41%, so money is moving out of the AI names rather than out of stocks altogether.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: oil prices a peace deal that hasn't happened. Secondary: AI doubts keep pulling down the Nasdaq.

OIL DEAL HOPES
Oil collapsed because traders are pricing a peace outcome before anyone has signed one.
Brent fell from $117.64 to $104.77 even though talks ended with no breakthrough and a report of US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island. Iran's statement that contacts continue was enough for traders to start removing the war premium (the extra price paid for the risk of supply being cut). The 10-year Treasury yield moved only 0.03 points lower, so bond buyers are not convinced this is lasting relief from inflation.
This drop is a bet on a deal, not a deal, so it can reverse quickly. A Brent close back above $110 would say the market has given up on the talks. If oil holds near $105, the inflation pressure the Fed worries about eases, but a December hike is still the base case until the data say otherwise.
AI DOUBT
Investors are still marking down the companies that spent the most on AI.
The Nasdaq lost another 1.07% to 25,164 on top of its 2.86% drop on October 30, and the VIX jumped. The Russell 2000 slipped just 0.21% and the Dow rose, which means the damage is confined to the biggest tech names. Fear that AI spending is outrunning AI revenue is a valuation problem, and neither cheaper oil nor lower yields can fix it.
Watch whether the Russell 2000 stays above 2,750. If it does, this is a big-tech repricing; if it breaks, the doubt has reached ordinary companies and the sell-off is broadening.
The Core Dynamic

Cheaper oil arrived and stocks couldn't use it

For weeks, oil was the obvious weight on stocks. Now that it has been lifted and the Nasdaq still fell, the real problem is whether AI profits can justify AI spending, and that is not an energy problem. That makes this sell-off harder to fix: a peace deal can cut oil, but it cannot make AI revenue appear.

Directional Read

The variable that decides the next move is whether the Iran talks turn into an actual agreement. If they do, oil stays below $110, yields have room to fall, and the AI question becomes the only thing stocks have to worry about. If they stall, oil snaps back and tech has to absorb the inflation problem and the profit doubts together. Today's oil drop is a bet on a deal, not a deal.

Scenario A — Deal firms up: Brent closes below $100 and the 10-year yield falls under 4.75%, showing the bond market also believes inflation pressure is easing.
Scenario B — Talks stall: Brent closes back above $110 while the Nasdaq slides below 24,500, pairing renewed inflation worry with deepening AI doubt.
