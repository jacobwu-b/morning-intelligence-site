Today's Thesis

A Blowout Jobs Report Revives the Rate-Hike Threat, and Tech Bounces Anyway

Employers added 165,000 jobs in October against forecasts of roughly 65,000, and that revived bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike in December. The dollar jumped 0.74% and gold fell 0.94%, the usual reaction when markets expect interest rates to go higher. Stocks still rose. The Nasdaq gained 1.09% and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) fell 9% to 25.64, but the Nasdaq is only recovering a fraction of Thursday's 5.52% rout. That makes this a snapback in tech, not a vote of confidence in the whole market. The Dow fell 0.46% and the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.86%, so the pressure from higher rates is still there and tech has only stopped falling for a day.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: strong jobs revive December hike odds. Secondary: tech's oversold bounce after Thursday's rout.

HIKE BETS RETURN
A jobs number more than 2.5 times the forecast has taken a December Fed rate hike from possibility toward expectation.
A labor market that is hiring slowly and firing rarely was supposed to cool inflation. Instead, payrolls beat forecasts by about 100,000 and unemployment edged down, so the Fed has less reason to hold off. The Fed was already leaning toward gradual hikes with inflation at 3.7%, and the dollar's 0.74% jump and gold's 0.94% drop show markets moving in that direction. Treasury yields barely moved (10-year at 4.86%, up 0.02 points), which suggests bond investors had already priced much of this in.
This is a durable driver, not a one-day reaction: unless inflation data cools, a December hike stays live, and anyone counting on the Fed to rescue stocks should assume it won't.
TECH SNAPBACK
After its worst day in 19 months, the Nasdaq bounced 1.09% as the most panicked selling eased.
The bounce looks like relief, not recovery. The Dow fell 0.46% while the Nasdaq led, so this was tech buyers returning to stocks that got hit hardest, not investors broadly embracing a stronger economy. The VIX fell 9% but at 25.64 still signals real nervousness. The cause of Thursday's selling, fear that a Democratic Congress and local data-center restrictions will slow AI building, has not been answered by anything today.
To know whether this is real, watch whether the Nasdaq can close above 25,000 while the 10-year yield stays under 5.00%. A close below 24,000 means investors are repricing AI, not just panicking.
The Core Dynamic

Good news on the economy is now a problem for the stocks that carry the market

Valuing an AI stock is like pricing a rental property by the rent it will collect years from now: the higher interest rates go, the less those distant rents are worth today. A strong jobs report helps near-term earnings but raises the rate that discounts everything far in the future, which is why tech can fall on strength and why today's bounce is fragile. In the usual version of a tech sell-off, the Fed's response is to ease. This time the Fed leans toward hiking, so tech has no cushion from the central bank while it deals with a political threat as well.

Historical Precedent

When rates reprice upward, the damage lands first on what was bought with cheap money

2013
In May 2013 the Fed hinted it would slow its bond buying, and the 10-year Treasury yield went from about 1.6% to nearly 3% by September. The S&P 500 fell roughly 6% between late May and late June, and emerging markets were hit harder. US stocks then recovered and made new highs because the economy was strong enough to absorb higher rates.
A rate scare is violent when it first hits, but it fades if growth is real.
1994
The Fed surprised markets by starting to raise rates in February 1994, taking its policy rate from 3% to 6% over about a year. The 10-year yield rose from roughly 5.7% to about 8% by late 1994, and bond markets suffered one of their worst years. The S&P 500 finished the year roughly flat, but leveraged and speculative investments broke along the way.
A hiking cycle does not need to crash the market to punish whoever relied on cheap money.
Directional Read

The variable that decides the next few days is the 10-year Treasury yield. If it holds under 5.00% while the Nasdaq works back toward 25,000, today's bounce can turn into stabilization. If strong data and sticky inflation push it through 5.00%, the tech rebound probably fails, because higher yields hurt growth stocks directly. Oil is not adding pressure for now: Brent held at $98.32 even with reports of missile strikes on an Iranian island. A strong economy no longer means the Fed will be gentle, so don't read a one-day bounce as the end of the sell-off.

Scenario A — Yields hold, tech stabilizes: The 10-year stays under 5.00% and the Nasdaq closes above 25,000, which would mean hike pricing is already in the price and Thursday's rout was an overreaction.
Scenario B — Rates break, tech breaks: The 10-year closes above 5.00% and the Nasdaq slips under 24,000, which would show investors are repricing AI and the Fed is no longer a backstop.
