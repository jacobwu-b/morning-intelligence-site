Today's Thesis

Inflation Just Locked In a Fed Hike, and the Bond Market Is Betting It Backfires

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge (the PCE index, which tracks what households actually pay) hit 3.8% over the past year, its highest since May and hotter than forecast. That all but guarantees a rate hike at the Fed's decision early next month. The new 20% tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China, Japan and the EU, add more price pressure on top. The surprise is that the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.85% (down 0.06 points) instead of rising. Bond buyers are treating a hike into a weakening economy as a growth risk, not an inflation cure. Stocks split on it: the Nasdaq fell 0.62% as the chip sell-off ground on, while the Dow and small caps rose.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: hot inflation plus tariffs forcing a Fed hike. Secondary: a credit-rating warning on Nvidia deepens the chip rout.

HOT INFLATION, NEW TARIFFS
A hotter-than-expected inflation reading has taken a pause off the table for the Fed.
October prices rose 3.8% from a year earlier on the Fed's preferred gauge, the highest since May. The new 20% tariffs make it worse, because importers typically pass those costs on to shoppers over the following months. Long-term yields still fell 0.06 points, which means bond buyers expect a hike to hit growth harder than it cools prices.
A hike next month is now the base case and a pause is the thing to bet against. The tariffs mean the next few inflation readings are unlikely to cool, so the pressure on the Fed lasts well beyond this print.
NVIDIA CREDIT WARNING
A warning that Nvidia's credit rating could be cut pushed the chip sell-off into a new phase.
S&P Global Ratings flagged "rising" management and governance risk at Nvidia, which is a statement about trust in the company, not about demand for its chips. That lands on top of the unresolved accounting probe and keeps the biggest AI name under a cloud. It also explains the split tape: investors sold the AI leaders while the Dow rose 0.33% and the Russell 2000 (small companies) rose 0.71%. Money is moving out of the crowded AI trade, not out of stocks altogether.
This is real as long as the accounting probe is open. An actual downgrade, or the Nasdaq breaking below its 22,618 low, would mean the sell-off has a second leg; quiet trading above that level would mean it is fading.
The Core Dynamic

A Fed Raising Rates Into a Slowing Economy

The Fed is tightening to fight prices while the market's growth engine, AI spending, is sputtering, much like braking hard on an icy road. That is why long-term yields fell on a hot print, when they would normally rise. The difference this time is that inflation is being fed from three sides at once (the hot reading, tariffs and oil near $95 Brent), so the Fed has less room to back off if growth cracks.

Directional Read

The variable that decides this is whether long-term yields start rising with inflation or keep falling on growth fear. Falling yields mean the bond market is the shock absorber and stocks can chop sideways. Rising yields alongside falling tech stocks would mean both major asset classes are repricing at once. Until yields rise, the hot inflation print is a story about the Fed, not yet a story about your portfolio.

Scenario A — Growth Fear Contains It: Nvidia avoids a downgrade, the 10-year yield stays near 4.85% or lower, and the Nasdaq holds above its 22,618 low as money keeps rotating into small caps and the Dow.
Scenario B — Stocks and Bonds Both Reprice: Yields turn up toward 5% on tariff and oil-driven inflation fears just as the Nvidia warning drags the Nasdaq back toward 22,618, and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) pushes past 30.
