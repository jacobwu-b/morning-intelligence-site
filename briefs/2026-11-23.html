Today's Thesis

One CEO's Return Sparked a 6.5% Nasdaq Snapback, and Fixed None of the Real Problems

The Nasdaq jumped 6.51% and the S&P 500 4.73%, more than erasing the previous session's rout. The trigger was the surprise return of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, which added about $950bn to the chipmaker's value in a single day (shares closed at $165.05). Because major indexes are weighted by company size, a near-trillion-dollar jump in one stock drags everything up with it, and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) fell 25.8% to 26.62. The move reads as relief, not new information. Nvidia is still about half a trillion dollars below its level before the episode, long-term Treasury yields barely moved, and forecasters are now talking about three straight Fed rate hikes. So treat today as the panic loosening its grip, not as an all-clear.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: Nvidia's CEO return fuels a relief squeeze. Secondary: Fed hike bets that stocks are ignoring.

NVIDIA RELIEF RALLY
A single stock's $950bn recovery turned a panic into a 6.5% Nasdaq rebound.
Nvidia is large enough that its one-day jump lifts every index that holds it, and the swing in sentiment likely forced investors who had bet against tech to buy back in. The rally also reached small companies, with the Russell 2000 up 3.4%, though it trailed the Nasdaq's 6.51% because big tech led. The White House publicly claimed credit for Huang's return, which adds a new risk: the market's most important AI company is now entangled with political involvement in its leadership.
A rally this violent shows how stretched the fear was, not that the cause is gone. Nvidia is still half a trillion dollars short of its old value and the accounting probe is unresolved, so the rebound holds only if Nvidia keeps its gains without fresh good news.
HIKE BETS IGNORED
Stocks soared on a day forecasters were calling for three straight Fed rate hikes starting next month.
The dollar rose 0.49% on that expectation, yet the 10-year yield sat at 4.92% and the 30-year slipped to 5.29%. That pairing is most consistent with bond buyers expecting hikes to slow growth rather than raise long-term borrowing costs. Falling oil (WTI down 4.54% to $80.17) eases some inflation pressure, but only the Fed's next price-gauge reading will say whether it is enough to change the December outlook.
The rebound has not priced a hike cycle, so a hot inflation reading from the Fed's preferred gauge (the PCE index, which investors are awaiting) would turn today's rally into a trap; a cool one would make a December hike the thing to bet against.
The Core Dynamic

Forced selling stops before fear does

A stretched rubber band snaps back hard when released, and that is what today was: the same forced cash-raising that crushed stocks and gold on the way down reversed on the way up, with gold gaining 3.87% alongside stocks. The difference from a normal recovery is that nothing underneath improved, with the Fed, AI valuations and Nvidia's accounting probe all still open, so the bounce is more fragile than its size suggests.

Directional Read

The deciding variable is whether the Nasdaq holds above its prior close of 22,618 over the next few sessions without another Nvidia headline. If it does, and gold holds its bounce, the forced-selling phase is likely over and the argument returns to inflation and the Fed. If it fails, today was a one-stock squeeze and the lows get retested. Don't mistake a 6.5% day for a verdict: a rally this large says the panic was real, and it can't tell you the problem is solved.

Scenario A — Relief becomes repair: The Nasdaq holds above 22,618 and Nvidia holds near $165 for several sessions while the 10-year yield stays below 5.00%, showing buyers are returning on fundamentals rather than a squeeze.
Scenario B — One-stock bounce fades: Nvidia gives back much of its jump, the Nasdaq slips back below 22,618, and the next inflation reading firms up December hike bets, pushing the 10-year yield toward 5.25%.
