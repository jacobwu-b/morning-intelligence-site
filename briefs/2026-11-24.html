Today's Thesis

Monday's Rebound Lost Its Legs: Money Is Leaving AI, Not Stocks

The Nasdaq fell 1.48% to 23,734, handing back about a quarter of Monday's 6.51% surge, while the Dow rose 0.85% and small caps gained 0.57%. Nvidia dropped 5% and Anthropic, the AI model developer, fell below the price it sold shares at in its IPO (its first public offering). Lawmakers in both parties are also objecting to how Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO was arranged, which adds a governance problem to the unresolved probe into the company's accounting. The VIX (the market's fear gauge) barely moved at 26.36, so this is a rotation out of the AI trade, not a new panic. Monday's bounce was relief, and it has not turned into a bottom.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: the AI rebound fades. Secondary: Houthi threats put a risk premium back into oil.

AI REBOUND FADES
Nothing new arrived to support Monday's Nvidia-led bounce, so buyers stepped back.
Nvidia fell 5% and Anthropic slid below its IPO price, dragging the Nasdaq down 1.48% while the Dow and small caps rose. Monday's surge came from one event, the CEO's return, and that event cannot be repeated. The accounting probe remains open, and objections from both parties to how Huang's return came about add a new political overhang. The practical effect is that the funds that fled tech are not coming back in size, and the money that did move is going to companies outside the AI story.
Expect choppy, lower-quality trading in AI names until Nvidia gets news on the probe; a Nasdaq close back under 22,618 would mean Monday's bounce was a trap.
OIL RISK PREMIUM
Houthi threats against Saudi ports and an alleged refinery attack pushed oil up more than 2%.
The market had been pricing steadily recovering Gulf supply, and today's headlines suggested that supply could be hit directly. Brent at $91.40 is still below the $95 level that would signal a real supply scare, so traders are paying for insurance, not reacting to missing barrels. That matters because higher fuel costs feed the inflation worry that has made every rate-hike bet harder to dismiss.
It is real only if barrels actually go missing; a Brent close above $95, or Saudi output confirmed lower, would make it so, and otherwise the premium fades within days.
The Core Dynamic

A relief rally is not a bottom

Monday's surge was a relief rally, a sharp bounce after panic selling stops, and those keep their gains only when something new supports them. Today nothing new arrived for AI stocks while oil gave inflation worry a fresh nudge, so money rotated toward companies outside the AI trade instead of leaving stocks altogether.

Directional Read

The variable that decides this week is the inflation data due later this week, because stocks have so far ignored the Fed's hike risk. A soft reading lets the rotation settle into a base; a hot one, with oil climbing, forces stocks to price hikes just as the AI trade is already weak. Until then, treat any single-day rally in AI names as a chance to see who is still buying, not as proof the worst has passed.

Scenario A — Soft inflation, steady Nvidia: Inflation comes in cooler than feared, oil stays under $90 for Brent, and the Nasdaq holds above 23,000 as the rotation calms.
Scenario B — Hot inflation, failed bounce: Inflation surprises high, Brent pushes above $95, and the Nasdaq closes below Monday's starting point of 22,618, which would put gold and tech selling back in lockstep.
