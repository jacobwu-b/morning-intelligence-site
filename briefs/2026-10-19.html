Today's Thesis

Tech Bounces Back, but the Lenders Haven't Said Yes Yet

The Nasdaq rose 1.11% to 26,431, winning back about half of Friday's 2.15% drop, and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) fell 7.75% to 18.44. It was a narrow bounce, not a broad one: the S&P 500 gained 0.41%, the Russell 2000 (small companies) just 0.25%, and the Dow slipped 0.07%. It came while the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.14%, so borrowing costs were not what held tech back last week and did not stop it today. The AI sell-off was about whether heavily indebted AI builders can keep financing themselves, and a one-day rebound in share prices does not answer that. Treat today as relief, not repair.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: a narrow tech rebound that hasn't fixed the AI lending worry. Secondary: oil stuck just under $110.

NARROW TECH BOUNCE
Big tech recovered half of Friday's drop, but nothing that caused the drop has been resolved.
The Nasdaq's 1.11% gain outran the S&P 500's 0.41%, and the Dow fell, so this was a handful of large names leading, not the market lifting together. Yields rose a little (the 10-year is at 5.14%) and tech still climbed, which tells you rates are not what is driving this episode. The driver is whether lenders keep funding AI companies that borrow heavily, and today's price action says nothing new about that.
Expect the bounce to hold only as long as no new lending scare appears. A Nasdaq close back below 25,500 would show the rebound was just a pause in the sell-off, while a push past 26,713 would be the first real sign the credit worry is easing.
OIL STALLS
Brent is pinned just under $110 while US crude catches up, and the war's costs are showing up in earnings.
Brent, the global benchmark, slipped to $109.55 while WTI, the US benchmark, jumped 2.22% to $103.66, shrinking the gap between them by about $2.50 a barrel. At these prices, companies exposed to the Iran war are feeling it directly: UK-listed firms issued 65 profit warnings in the three months to the end of September, the most in two years. Higher energy costs are therefore reaching company earnings, not just fuel prices. A Brent close at $110 or higher would end the stall; a close below $100 would be real relief.
Oil near $110 keeps pressure on the Fed to stay hawkish and on company profits, so watch whether WTI keeps closing the gap while Brent holds below $110.
The Core Dynamic

Stocks can bounce in a day, but credit trust takes weeks to rebuild

A share price is a daily mood, while a lender's willingness to fund a heavily indebted company changes slowly. That is why a bounce that arrives with yields still climbing reads as relief, not an all-clear.

Directional Read

The variable that decides the week is whether the AI lending worry stays quiet while the 10-year yield sits above 5.1%. If it does, the Nasdaq can keep recovering toward its prior level. If lenders flinch again, today's gain disappears fast, because nothing has actually been fixed. A bounce after a credit scare is not a turn until the lenders agree.

Scenario A — Credit calm holds: The Nasdaq closes above 26,713 with the 10-year yield below 5.20%, showing the sell-off was a one-off scare.
Scenario B — Lenders flinch again: New spikes in bond yields on the heavily indebted AI names push the Nasdaq back under 25,500 and the VIX toward 25.
