Today's Thesis

Big Tech Takes the Wheel Back, Stopping Ten Points Short of the Line That Matters

The Nasdaq rose 1.05% to 26,703, just 10 points under the 26,713 level that has been the test of whether the AI rally is back. The S&P 500 gained 0.58%, and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) fell to 16.68. Yesterday's rotation into smaller companies reversed: the Russell 2000 rose only 0.27%, so big tech led and small caps lagged. Stocks rose even as the 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to 5.15%, which tells you investors are treating today's borrowing costs as a price they can pay, not a wall. The one thing that complicates the calm is oil, where the two main benchmarks moved in opposite directions.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: tech reclaims leadership as the VIX drops. Secondary: Brent and WTI split apart.

TECH REBOUND
Money moved back into the biggest growth names, and the market stopped charging extra for fear.
The Nasdaq's 1.05% gain beat the Russell 2000's 0.27%, reversing yesterday's rotation into smaller companies, and the VIX fell 3.64%. A $15 billion fundraise by DeepSeek, the Chinese AI lab, is a reminder that money is still being offered to AI builders, which likely helped sentiment. Yields rose slightly (10-year up 0.03 points to 5.15%) and did no damage.
A close above 26,713 would confirm tech has regained the lead, but only while the 10-year stays under about 5.20%; above that, today's gain looks like a bounce inside a range, not a new trend.
OIL SPLIT
The world's oil price held near a five-month high while the American one dropped almost 5%.
Brent, the global benchmark, closed flat at $108.82 after touching a five-month high on the escalating Middle East conflict. WTI, the US benchmark, fell 4.56% to $97.15, so the gap between the two is now about $11.70, up from about $7 yesterday. The most likely explanation is that US-specific supply is easing while barrels shipped by sea stay scarce.
If the gap stays above $10 and Brent breaks $110, this is a global shipping and supply squeeze that American relief won't fix; if the gap closes because Brent falls, that would be the first real sign of easing.
The Core Dynamic

The whole world is paying more to borrow, and stocks are leaning on that not getting worse

UK inflation rose to 3.5%, UK government debt passed £3 trillion, and French government bond yields hit their highest since 2002. The US 30-year yield at 5.44% is part of the same tide, so US stocks can keep rising only while that tide stops short of a new surge.

Directional Read

The variable that decides this is the 30-year Treasury yield, which sets the price of long-term money for everything from AI data centers to mortgages. If it holds near 5.44%, the Nasdaq has a clear path through 26,713. If European bond stress pushes it toward 5.50%, today's gain probably fades. Treat 26,713 as a reading on borrowing costs, not just a tech price.

Scenario A — Breakout: The Nasdaq closes above 26,713 while the 10-year stays at or below 5.15% and the VIX stays under 17, showing tech can lead without help from lower rates.
Scenario B — Bond stress spreads: The 30-year yield climbs to 5.50% or more as European bond selling continues and Brent clears $110, squeezing both tech valuations and household budgets at once.
