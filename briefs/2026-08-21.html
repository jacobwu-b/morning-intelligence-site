Today's Thesis

Stocks Rallied Today — Gold And Bitcoin Rallied Harder, And That's The Real Signal

The S&P rose 0.43% and the Dow climbed 0.98% today, but the more important move happened underneath: gold and bitcoin both surged in the same session that the government bond selloff — now in its fourth day — deepened further. That's not a contradiction, it's a tell. When investors buy stocks with one hand and hard assets with the other during a bond rout, they aren't calling the all-clear on government debt — they're hedging against it while still trying to participate in the market. Combine that with the national debt clearing $40 trillion this week and June's tariff refunds erasing more than $25 billion in net customs revenue the government was counting on, and the fiscal backdrop behind this selloff looks less like a glitch and more like a structural problem the market is starting to price permanently.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary driver: the debasement trade widens beneath a rising stock market. Secondary: the Fed's own communication becomes a source of volatility.

DEBASEMENT TRADE
Gold and bitcoin are surging because investors no longer trust the fix for the debt problem, not because they're fleeing stocks.
The Treasury's buyback program — where it repurchases its own older bonds to support prices — bought one calm session earlier this week, then the selloff came right back. Today, rather than dumping stocks, investors bought gold and bitcoin on top of equities, which only makes sense if they're treating government debt itself as the risk to hedge against. The debt crossing $40 trillion and a $25 billion tariff-refund shortfall hitting customs revenue in June are both proof points that the fiscal trajectory, not a temporary bond-market plumbing issue, is what's driving this.
This doesn't get fixed by another buyback announcement — it gets fixed by the deficit outlook improving or by the Fed re-establishing that it controls the inflation story. Neither looks close, so expect gold and bitcoin to keep acting as the pressure gauge on days when stocks look calm.
FED FOG
Goldman's top economist says the Fed's vaguer new communication style is amplifying every market swing.
The warning is specifically that the Fed has pulled back from clear forward guidance — telling markets less about its future rate path — right as global bond markets are already jumpy, which means every incoming data point now gets over-interpreted because investors have less to anchor expectations to. Whether this is noise or signal depends on what happens next: if the Fed clarifies its reaction function and daily swings shrink, this was a temporary rough patch; if every future data print keeps producing outsized yield moves, the Fed itself has become part of the instability.
Watch whether daily yield swings shrink or grow over the next several sessions — that tells you whether Hatzius's warning was a one-time complaint or the start of a pattern.
The Core Dynamic

The Hedge And The Bet Are Happening At The Same Time

Think of a homeowner who keeps making mortgage payments on time while quietly buying insurance against their own house losing value — that's what the market is doing right now: still buying stocks (the house), while simultaneously buying gold and bitcoin (the insurance) against the debt underneath everything. The two trades aren't in conflict — they're the same trade, split across two assets because no single safe haven feels safe enough. That's what makes this harder than a normal flight-to-quality moment: usually there's one obvious place to hide (Treasuries), but this time the thing everyone would normally hide in is the thing under suspicion, so the money is scattering instead of consolidating.

Historical Precedent

Gold's Rise Can Mean Two Very Different Things — Structural Distrust Or A Speculative Squeeze

1971
When Nixon closed the gold window and ended the dollar's direct convertibility to gold, it wasn't a single event but the start of a decade-long erosion of confidence — gold rose from $35 an ounce to over $800 by 1980 as investors gradually concluded the dollar's backing had permanently weakened.
Once a currency or debt credibility crack opens, gold's rise tends to outlast any single policy fix aimed at closing it.
1980
The Hunt brothers' attempt to corner the silver market drove prices from roughly $6 to nearly $50 an ounce in months — a parabolic move that collapsed by more than half within weeks once exchanges raised margin requirements and forced liquidation.
A hard-asset rally driven by speculative momentum can reverse just as violently as it built, unlike one driven by genuine loss of faith in the system it's hedging against.
Directional Read

The variable that decides which of today's two stories wins is simple: does the Treasury yield — the government's own borrowing cost — stabilize, or does it keep climbing? If it stabilizes, today's gold and bitcoin surge looks like a hedge that gets unwound as confidence returns and stocks keep the rally to themselves. If yields keep rising, the hard-asset move stops being a side story and becomes the main one, because higher government borrowing costs eventually flow into mortgage rates, corporate debt costs, and ultimately stock valuations themselves. Hold this: right now the market is betting both ways at once, and that split can't last indefinitely.

Scenario A — Yields Stabilize: If the 10-year yield stops climbing and holds steady for several sessions, the gold/bitcoin surge fades as a hedge that's no longer needed, and stocks keep grinding higher on their own.
Scenario B — Selloff Compounds: If yields keep rising alongside gold and bitcoin, the debasement trade stops being a side bet and starts pulling equity valuations down as borrowing costs bite into earnings and mortgages.
