Today's Thesis

The Consumer Didn't Get the Memo About Stagflation

Growth slowed to a 1.5% annual rate in the spring, down from 2.1% the quarter before — the headline bears expected. But underneath it, consumer spending actually picked up steam, and that's the number that mattered today. The S&P jumped 1.66%, the Nasdaq surged 2.78%, and the Dow rose 1.19%, all recovering ground lost yesterday when Warsh's hawkish Fed hold and Meta's spending shock hit stocks together. Consumers are two-thirds of the US economy — if they keep spending through slower growth and a hawkish Fed, the recession leg of the stagflation story gets pushed out, even if the inflation leg is still very much alive.

What's Actually Driving This

Resilient consumer spending sparks a broad relief rally; tech leads the bounce back from yesterday's Meta-driven rout

CONSUMER HOLDS UP
GDP growth cooled to 1.5%, but Americans spent more, not less.
The slowdown from 2.1% growth last quarter to 1.5% this quarter is real, and trade deficits and inflation are both squeezing the numbers. But consumer spending — the engine that actually determines whether a slowdown turns into a recession — accelerated instead of cracking, which is the opposite of what a full stagflation scenario requires.
This is one data point covering a quarter that's already over — it tells you where the economy was, not where it's going with oil back near $90 and rates held high. Watch tomorrow's inflation reading and next week's jobs report before trusting that consumer resilience survives a live energy shock with no rate relief.
TECH SNAPS BACK
The Nasdaq's 2.78% gain reversed yesterday's Meta-led tech rout almost in full.
Yesterday the selloff was broad enough that the Dow fell harder than the Nasdaq — a sign the damage was macro, not just an AI-valuation story. Today's snapback, with tech leading gains, suggests investors are treating Meta's spending shock as company-specific rather than proof the whole AI capital-spending trade is broken.
Apple and Amazon report this week. If either echoes Meta's aggressive-spending-without-clear-return story, today's relief reverses fast; if they show spending discipline, the bounce has legs.
The Core Dynamic

Two Competing Stories, One Market

Markets are re-pricing daily whether slowing growth means recession or just moderation — yesterday said recession risk plus sticky inflation, today said moderation with a resilient consumer. Both can be true simultaneously, and that unresolved tension, not a clean trend, is what's whipsawing prices day to day.

Directional Read

The variable that decides which story wins is tomorrow's inflation data, landing with oil back near $90 a barrel. If inflation cools despite the energy spike, Warsh gets room to soften without losing credibility and today's rally has a foundation. If inflation reaccelerates, the Fed is boxed in — hold rates against a softening economy — and today looks like a bounce, not a turn.

Scenario A — Inflation Cools Despite Oil: Tomorrow's inflation reading comes in soft even with oil near $90, giving Warsh room to signal eventual relief without abandoning his inflation-fighting stance.
Scenario B — Energy Pass-Through Hits: Inflation data reaccelerates on energy costs, forcing Warsh to hold longer against weakening growth, and today's rally is remembered as the bounce before the next leg down.
