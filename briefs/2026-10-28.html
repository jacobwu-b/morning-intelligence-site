Today's Thesis

The Fed Didn't Cancel the Hike — It Rescheduled It for December

The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate steady in a split vote (not unanimous), but investors read it as a delay, not a stop: another increase in December is widely expected with inflation still running at 3.7%. Stocks edged up (S&P 500 +0.28%) because there was no fresh shock, and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) fell 3.5% to 16.91. The bond market gave the opposite answer: the 10-year Treasury yield rose 0.05 points to 5.05%, back above the 5% line, and the 30-year rose to 5.36%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said politics played no role, with the midterms less than a week away. Stocks are calm because the pain was pushed back, and the bond market is charging for it anyway.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: a Fed hold that signals a December hike. Secondary: oil rebounding and feeding the inflation the Fed is fighting.

FED POSTPONES
The Fed paused for one meeting, and bond investors are charging as if the hiking isn't over.
The split vote shows the committee is not unified on how much more to tighten, but inflation at 3.7% leaves little room to ease. Stocks rose because a hold removed an immediate threat, and the fear gauge slipped. Longer-term bonds sold off instead: the 10-year at 5.05% and the 30-year at 5.36% show lenders pricing more tightening and stubborn inflation ahead.
The relief is real but borrowed against December. Don't count on rate cuts, and treat any stock gain that comes with the 10-year above 5% as fragile until the Nasdaq proves it can clear 26,713.
OIL REBOUNDS
Crude jumped back, with US oil rising faster than the global benchmark.
WTI (the US benchmark) rose 2.88% to $102.78 and Brent (the global benchmark) rose 1.26% to $115.32, reversing the prior session's dip. Fuel is a cost that feeds through to nearly everything else, which is why oil near these levels keeps inflation stubborn and the December hike on the table. Because WTI rose more, the gap with Brent narrowed to about $12.54.
Brent closing under $110 would ease the inflation case for a December hike; a close at $120 would make that hike close to unavoidable.
The Core Dynamic

The Fed is fighting an oil-driven inflation that rate hikes can't fix

Higher interest rates cool demand, but they do nothing to bring back barrels of oil, so the Fed keeps threatening hikes until fuel prices turn. That is why a calm stock market can sit on top of a restless bond market.

Directional Read

The variable that decides this is the 10-year yield against the 5% line, and whether the Nasdaq can keep rising through it. If tech clears 26,713 with the yield holding near 5%, markets are accepting a December hike as old news. If the yield pushes toward 5.20% and tech starts falling, the hold will look like the last calm before another rate increase. The Fed didn't cancel the hike, it moved it to December.

Scenario A — Yields stall, tech breaks out: The 10-year holds near 5% and the Nasdaq closes above 26,713, showing markets can absorb a December hike.
Scenario B — Yields climb, tech cracks: The 10-year pushes to 5.20% while the Nasdaq drops 1% or more, and the slide toward 25,500 begins.
