Today's Thesis

Stocks Hit Records on a Few AI Giants While Borrowing Costs Stay Punishing

The S&P 500 rose 0.58% to 7,818.93, its first close above 7,800, as Nvidia and Microsoft pulled the Nasdaq (+0.45%) and the Dow (+0.49%) higher. The rally is narrow. The Russell 2000, an index of smaller US companies, fell 0.59% on the same day. The 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark rate that sets borrowing costs across the economy, eased to 5.27% (-0.04 pts) but is still above the 5.25% line we have been tracking. A handful of AI companies is carrying the index to records while the cost of money stays high. That works only as long as their earnings keep growing faster than the rate you can earn on a risk-free bond.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: an AI-led rally running into 5%+ bond yields. Secondary: oil stuck near $100 as Gulf supply recovers slowly.

AI VS YIELDS
A few giant AI stocks are doing the work of the whole market while bonds offer a rich, safe alternative.
Nvidia and Microsoft drove the move, pushing the S&P 500 past 7,800 for the first time. Smaller companies fell 0.59% on the same day, because they tend to carry more debt that resets at current high rates and have no AI profits to offset it. The 10-year yield slipped to 5.27%, a small relief but still above the 5.25% level we are watching. The Fed is still debating a rate increase, not a cut.
This holds as long as AI earnings keep surprising upward and the 10-year stays below 5.50%. A close at 5.50% or higher would force investors to ask why they should own richly priced stocks when a Treasury pays that much with no risk. A narrow rally on top of a high-yield backdrop can last for weeks, but it has few places to hide if either leg weakens.
OIL STUCK
Brent crude is hovering at $100 because supply is returning slowly and the war is not settled.
Brent closed at $100.58 (+0.26%), and Gulf flows averaged only 81% of the pre-war rate in September. Vice President JD Vance said Iran must cut enrichment to end the war. Fighting also continues in Yemen, and new US sanctions threaten foreign banks that deal with Iran. Each of these keeps supply risk alive even as barrels trickle back. Oil at $100 keeps fuel costs, and therefore inflation, elevated, which is what stops the Fed from easing.
Real relief would be October Gulf flows reported above 90% of the pre-war rate and Brent closing under $95 two sessions in a row. Until then, treat $100 oil as a floor under inflation worries, not a passing spike.
The Core Dynamic

Stocks have to beat a savings account that pays 5%

Think of owning stocks like owning a rental property. If a risk-free savings account pays 5.27%, the property has to offer much faster growth to justify its price. When safe yields are this high, a stock market at record highs needs earnings growth to do the work that cheap money used to do. Only a few AI companies are delivering that growth, which is why smaller companies fell today while the big indexes rose. What makes this episode different from the usual record-high rally is that yields are rising or high, not falling, so the market is more fragile and depends on fewer stocks.

Historical Precedent

Stocks can ignore climbing yields for a while, but history says they rarely get to ignore them for good

1994
The Fed doubled short-term rates over 1994 and the 10-year Treasury yield rose from roughly 5.8% to about 8% by November. The S&P 500 finished the year roughly flat rather than crashing. The damage landed in bonds and in leveraged bond bets, most visibly in Orange County, California's bankruptcy that December.
A flat stock market during a bond rout can hide where the damage is gathering.
2023
The 10-year yield pushed up to about 5% in October 2023, and the S&P 500 fell roughly 10% from its July high to its late-October low. Stocks bottomed as yields turned lower, then rallied hard into year end.
Stocks recovered when yields stopped rising, not when earnings improved.
Directional Read

The variable that decides this is the 10-year yield. If it stays near 5.25%-5.30%, AI earnings can keep carrying the indexes. If it pushes toward 5.50%, even great earnings will struggle to justify these prices. A record high on a 5%+ bond yield is a market that is betting on a handful of companies, so watch the bond market before you trust the rally.

Scenario A — Yields ease, AI keeps winning: The 10-year drifts back under 5.25% and Gulf flows keep climbing, so the Fed stays on hold and the rally broadens beyond tech to smaller companies.
Scenario B — Yields break higher: The 10-year closes at 5.50% or higher while Brent stays near $100, the Fed leans toward a hike, and the narrow AI rally loses its footing.
