Today's Thesis

Tech Rebounds as AI's Funding Starts Moving From Borrowing to Selling Stock

The Nasdaq rose 0.82% to 26,169 and the S&P 500 gained 0.7% as AI's money problem changed shape. Meta announced a $95 billion fundraising drive built around a share sale, and Anthropic, the AI model developer, filed for an IPO (its first sale of stock to the public). With the 10-year Treasury yield stuck at 4.99%, borrowing is expensive, so the biggest AI spenders are turning to investors for ownership money instead of lenders for loans. That eased the credit-stress fear behind last week's tech selloff, and the VIX (the market's fear gauge) fell 5.45% to 18.39. The relief is conditional: stock buyers now have to absorb a flood of new shares, and the Fed's decision this week still hangs over every rate-sensitive position.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: AI funding pivots from loans to stock sales. Secondary: oil dips on diplomacy hopes

AI FUNDING PIVOT
The AI build-out's enormous cash needs are moving from the bond market to the stock market.
With the 10-year at 4.99% and the 30-year at 5.30%, borrowing billions for chips and data centers is costly, and last week's selloff showed investors marking tech down for how it is financed. Meta's $95 billion drive, built around a share sale, and Anthropic's IPO filing are the other route: selling ownership carries no interest bill and no repayment date. The cost is dilution (each existing share becomes a smaller slice) and a need for buyers willing to pay today's high valuations.
This lowers the odds of a debt accident in tech but raises the amount of new stock the market must digest, so the rally is real only if the Nasdaq reclaims 26,713 while that supply arrives. Until then, favor companies funding AI from their own cash flow over those that need outside money.
OIL ON DIPLOMACY
Hopes for Middle East diplomacy trimmed oil, but attacks on Saudi Arabia kept the dip shallow.
Brent fell 0.78% to $115.81 and WTI fell 1.46% to $101.76, which eased some of the inflation pressure that keeps the Fed leaning toward a hike. But Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Saudi Arabia were intercepted, not absent, and gold rose 0.76% with the dollar, which is not what a fully relaxed market does. A dip this small on talk alone is the kind that fades quickly.
Oil is a diplomacy headline away from reversing, so treat Brent at $110 as the level that would show real de-escalation and $120 as the level that shows it has failed.
The Core Dynamic

When Borrowing Gets Too Expensive, Companies Sell Pieces of Themselves

It is like a homeowner who can't afford a 5% mortgage and instead sells a share of the house to cover the renovation. The roof gets fixed and the lender is spared, but the owner now owns less of the house, and it only works while someone keeps paying a high price for that share.

Directional Read

The variable that decides this week is whether buyers keep absorbing AI stock supply at current prices while the Fed signals its next move. If the Nasdaq reclaims 26,713 on calm bond yields, today was the start of a repair; if yields push higher or the new shares meet weak demand, it was only a pause. A rally that depends on investors paying up for fresh AI stock can't survive a hawkish Fed.

Scenario A — Equity funding works: The Nasdaq closes above 26,713 with the 10-year still near 4.99% and the VIX below 18, showing buyers are absorbing new AI shares without a rate shock.
Scenario B — Supply meets a hawkish Fed: The Fed signals more hikes, the 10-year pushes above 5.10%, and the Nasdaq slides back toward 25,500 as new AI share sales find fewer buyers.

What to Watch
The Fed announces another rate increase at its decision this week. — A second hike would confirm that inflation at 3.7% outweighs credit worries and make expensive AI financing harder to defend.
The 10-year Treasury yield closes at or above 5.10%. — A break above the roughly 5% level it has hovered around would raise the cost of AI borrowing and challenge the rebound in tech.
The VIX closes below 16. — It would show the fear behind last week's tech selloff has largely faded and that equity funding is being welcomed.
Brent closes at least $15 above WTI. — A wider gap would mean tight seaborne supply is outweighing the diplomacy-driven dip in US crude, keeping the inflation pressure intact.
