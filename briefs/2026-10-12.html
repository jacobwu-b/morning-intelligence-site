Today's Thesis

Oil Is Climbing Back Above $105 While Stocks Hold Their Breath at the Record

Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, rose 1.84% to $106.65, and US crude rose 2.06% to $93.74. The likely trigger is a fresh round of attacks on Saudi airports, which forced Qatar Airways to suspend flights to Riyadh and Dammam. That undercuts the story that Gulf supply is steadily healing. The bond market absorbed it without flinching: the 10-year Treasury yield sat unchanged at 5.24%, but that is no comfort, because a yield this high is already pricing a Fed that keeps raising rates. The S&P 500 slipped 0.06%, just 0.15% under its record, while the Nasdaq fell 0.44% and the Dow rose 0.38%. Oil that stays above $105 feeds into this week's US inflation data, and every hot reading makes a rate cut harder to imagine.

What's Actually Driving This

Primary: Gulf attacks push oil back above $105. Secondary: tech cools while yields stay pinned above 5.2%.

OIL RE-ESCALATION
Attacks on Gulf airports are reminding traders that the supply recovery is fragile.
Brent gained $1.93 on the day to $106.65 and WTI to $93.74, pushing oil back above the $105 line. The war has no settlement, and a promise from Trump to hold off on striking Iran before the midterms does nothing to stop attacks by others. Oil is the cleanest link between the war and the Fed: higher fuel costs flow into inflation numbers, and those numbers decide whether the Fed hikes again.
Oil is not yet a trend, but a second close above $105 would show buyers are paying up for the risk and would make a hot inflation report more likely. A Brent close back under $100 would show today was a scare, not a new leg.
TECH BEHIND YIELDS
The chip-heavy Nasdaq slipped while the Dow rose, as a yield above 5.2% keeps weighing on expensive stocks.
The 10-year did not move, but it did not need to. Money that can earn 5.24% from the US government without taking equity risk demands a lot from AI stocks priced for years of growth. A stronger dollar (+0.36%) and weaker gold (-0.61%) fit the same picture: investors are choosing yield over hedges.
With the S&P 500 just 0.15% below its record, one calm day could set a new high. Whether it holds there depends on this week's inflation data, because yields have no room to climb further without hurting stocks.
The Core Dynamic

Oil is the bridge between the war and your borrowing costs

Every dollar added to oil lifts fuel and shipping costs, which show up in inflation, which keeps the Fed leaning toward higher rates and the 10-year stuck above 5.2%. The stock market is holding near a record at a price for money it has not had to pay in years. Today's twist is that bond yields didn't budge on the oil jump, so the damage showed up in tech stocks instead.

Directional Read

The variable that decides this week is whether oil's jump leaks into the inflation data. If it does not, yields stay put and the S&P 500 is likely to test its record. If it does, a 10-year already at 5.24% has room to climb and tech is the likely casualty. With oil above $105 and yields above 5.2%, the market has little margin left for an inflation surprise.

Scenario A — Oil scare fades: Brent slides back under $100 as Gulf flights resume and this week's inflation reading comes in tame, letting the S&P 500 close above 7,818.93.
Scenario B — Oil feeds inflation: Brent pushes toward $110 and the inflation data runs hot, sending the 10-year toward 5.40% and the Nasdaq into a sharp drop.
