Today's Thesis

Markets Rally Because the Fed Didn't Surprise Anyone

Stocks jumped across the board today — the S&P 500 up 1.14%, the Nasdaq up 1.69% — a day after the Federal Reserve delivered the interest rate hike everyone already expected. The rally isn't about the inflation problem getting solved; it's about two sources of uncertainty resolving cleanly: the hike came through unanimous with no dissent, and oil prices fell the same day, easing the specific fear that's been driving this whole inflation story. Layered on top, President Trump revealed he personally told Fed Chair Kevin Warsh to vote for the hike because other officials would anyway — a more direct claim of influence over Fed policy than his earlier rhetoric alone, and one markets shrugged off today but shouldn't ignore going forward.

What's Actually Driving This

Hike-relief and falling oil drove the rally; Trump's admission on Warsh's vote is the story to watch next

RELIEF RALLY
The market cheered because the worst-case hike scenario didn't happen.
The Fed's hike arrived unanimous, with a clear signal of one more hike this year — no surprise size, no dissent, no ambiguity. Falling oil prices on the same day removed the second overhang, easing the specific inflation fear that's been driving this entire story. Markets often rally hardest not when a problem is solved, but when the range of bad outcomes narrows — that's what happened today, not evidence the inflation fight is over.
This kind of relief rally tends to last only as long as the next data point cooperates — watch whether oil keeps falling and yields hold their ease, because if either reverses, the relief unwinds just as fast as it arrived.
INDEPENDENCE WATCH
Trump just claimed direct influence over a Fed vote, and markets didn't blink.
Trump said he told Warsh to vote for the hike because other officials were going to anyway — combined with his continued public demand for rates near zero, that's a more concrete claim of involvement than jawboning alone. Today's rally shows markets are treating this as noise for now, not a credibility problem for the Fed's inflation fight. That verdict changes fast if Trump moves from claiming influence to trying to remove or override officials directly.
Watch whether this stays at the level of comments after the fact, or escalates into concrete attempts to control future votes — the second is what would spook the bond market regardless of what the data says.
The Core Dynamic

The relief is about uncertainty resolving, not the inflation problem disappearing.

Today's rally cleared two specific overhangs — the hike's size and oil's direction — but the underlying tension between a Fed fighting supply-driven inflation and a president who wants rates near zero is still fully live, just quieter for a day.

Directional Read

The variable that decides whether this rally holds is whether today's twin reliefs — an unsurprising hike and falling oil — turn into a trend or reverse within days. If oil keeps sliding and the next yield print eases, the stagflation trade that's dominated for over 100 days genuinely loses its grip. If oil snaps back or Trump's pressure on the Fed escalates past rhetoric, today's gains look like a one-day pause in a much harder story. Hold this one thought through the week: relief rallies are a bet on what happens next, not a verdict on what already happened.

Scenario A — De-escalation Confirms: Oil keeps falling toward $100 and the next Treasury yield close drops back under 5%, confirming both the Iran risk premium and the inflation fear are genuinely easing.
Scenario B — Pressure Escalates: Trump's comments about directing Fed votes intensify into real attempts to control future decisions, spooking bond markets into pushing yields back above 5.25% regardless of what oil does.
